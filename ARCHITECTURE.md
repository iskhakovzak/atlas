# Atlas architecture

## Home sheets — 5 October 2026

The owner asked for the home page to read as separate full-screen sheets: one part, then the next, with a flick snapping to the next sheet "like a magnet", and nothing of the next block peeking at the bottom of a screen.

- **Where.** `app/home-chapters.css`, imported last in `app/layout.tsx` (after `day-folio.css`). No `!important`, no entrance or scroll-driven animations, no inner scroll box, no `dvh`, no `:is()` with ids, no DOM moves by script.
- **Scope.** Root rules use `html:has(main.catalog-home)`; home rules `html:has(main.catalog-home) .catalog-home …` (specificity 0,2,2, above the Day/Night 0,2,1 rules); sheet lists use `:where(...)`. Browsers without `:has()` get no snapping and the normal layout. Other routes keep `scroll-snap-type: none` (also after client-side navigation away from `/`).
- **Snapping.** The root scroller (`html`) has `scroll-snap-type: y mandatory` under `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`, `y proximity` when the window is 700px tall or less (Safari with toolbars out, ~690px laptop windows, landscape), and none under reduced motion (the sheet layout stays). `scroll-snap-stop` stays normal. A commented `(hover:hover) and (pointer:fine)` proximity fallback is in the file for a real Windows wheel check. Snap targets (`scroll-snap-align: start`, `scroll-margin-top: 0`): `.site-header` (sheet 1 starts at y=0 with the static header), `#bill` (below 1100px), `#how`, `#finds`, `#tariffs`, `#trust` (761px and up) or `.home-trust-money` + `.home-trust-proof` (760px and below), `#faq`, `.home-end`. `scroll-padding-bottom` is the phone bar + dock.
- **Tokens** on the root: `--ch-header` (80px; 60px at ≤600), `--ch-nav` (0; 61px + safe area at ≤760, the measured `.mobile-nav`), `--ch-dock` (0; 68px at ≤760), `--ch-pad` (`clamp(32px,6svh,72px)`; `clamp(16px,3svh,32px)` on phones, 14px on phones ≤840px tall, `clamp(20px,3svh,32px)` on wide windows ≤860px tall), `--ch-gap`, `--ch-h1 = 100svh − header − nav` (sheet 1) and `--ch-h = 100svh − nav − dock` (`100lvh` at ≤760, so the next heading does not show when Safari's toolbars collapse). Sheets use `min-height`, never a fixed height or `overflow:hidden`; a sheet whose content is taller (an opened FAQ answer) grows.
- **Sheets, phones (≤760).** 1 header + hero (`--ch-h1`; ends exactly at the bar) with a quiet "Пример счёта ⌄" cue (`.home-next-bill`, anchor to `#bill`); 2 the example bill; 3 how it works; 4 product selection; 5 rates as 2-column tiles; 6 money facts; 7 order tracking (+ parcel photos, reviews, legal when filled); 8 FAQ; 9 end (footer only; the closing card stays hidden on phones as before). 761–1099px is the same order with the bill folio at most 640px wide and the rates as a table.

The end sheet is one full screen tall and reaches the document bottom, so the last snap lands exactly on it; on phones its bottom padding keeps the footer clear of the dock and bar. `main.catalog-home` has no bottom padding.

## Simpler bill, customs paid through Atlas, checkout to the order — 5 October 2026

- **No international reserve.** `tariff.reserve` is 0 and `upgradePricing` (`pricingRevision` 3) sets 0 on saved tariffs and drops `reserve` from country overrides. The quote field stays (0) and older orders keep theirs; `settle()` refunds or asks for consent as before. Bills hide the reserve line, the example row and the blank-bill row while the rate is 0; an operator can still set one in Тарифы, and it then shows as its own line.
- **Customs paid through Atlas.** `Pricing.customsHelpFee` defaults to 0.0498 (was 0.03 of goods, a request outside the order). `repriceCart(items, now, config, customsHelp)` adds `quote.customsHelp` = round(line `merchandise` × rate, delivery excluded) and `customsHelpRate` to every line when `state.cartCustoms.help` is set; `addToCart`, `changeQuantity`, `renewCart`, `cart-remove` pass `customsHelpChosen(state)`, and `setCartCustoms` reprices when the choice changes (new quote IDs, so the confirmed signature changes). `checkoutCart` refuses a cart whose lines do not match the choice, adds the estimated duty for the chosen recipient (`ceil(estimateUsd × fx)`, split by goods value) as `quote.customsDuty` inside `total` — the `checkout` action carries the `customsDuty` the confirmation step showed and a different figure is refused — notes both in each order's history and clears `cartCustoms`. `confirmCustomsDuty` (operator, `confirm-customs-duty`) records `customsSettlement`: a lower duty credits the rest to the balance at once, a higher one waits for `approveCustomsExtra` (customer); an order with the fee cannot be marked delivered until the duty is settled. The duty is transit in the books (in `payable`, not in revenue); the projection adds a `customs_duty` fee line. The books count the fee as service income (`orderFinance.services`); the projection adds a `customs_help` fee line. `cartCustomsEstimate` records `helpRequested` whenever chosen and `helpFeeUsd` from the quotes.
- **Customs panel** (`app/calc-summary.tsx` `CustomsPanel`): the allowance in two lines, the duty estimate when the cart is over it, the recipient picker, and the "Atlas pays customs for me" checkbox (cart and review step) with its amount. The "already over the limit outside Atlas" checkbox is no longer shown; `cartCustoms.outsideUsed` stays optional in the schema and older carts still count it.
- **Link order.** Unknown store delivery from a store order above `storeShippingFreeFromUsd` shows 0 in a read-only field (the stored hold estimate is unchanged and applies again if the order drops below the threshold). The customs block moved into the bill sheet; the "not in the amount" slip shows only a store-delivery hold.
- **Checkout.** After `checkout` succeeds the cart waits for the new orders and goes to `/orders#<first order>`, which opens that order with its payment step. The "Предзаказ оформлен" dialog and the in-cart demo payment are gone; the order card keeps "Отметить оплату в Atlas". A payment provider's redirect belongs between the two once one is connected.

## Security audit fixes — 5 October 2026

- **`import-legacy` removed.** The action took client JSON as the whole account document, so any signed-in customer with an empty profile could write their own balance (checked: 50,000,000 soum), paid orders and staff fields. It is gone from `actionSchema` (`lib/market/actions.ts`); an old client sending it gets 400. No action replaces the account document wholesale.
- **Rate limits.** `rateLimit(name,max,windowMs,code)` in `lib/market/server.ts` counts in `market_rate_limits` (fixed window). `/api/actions`: 60 actions per minute per account (`err_20`), and of those `cart-add`, `cart-add-many`, `cart-check` and `checkout`, which ask the stores through the proxy, 40 per 10 minutes (`err_39`). `/api/passport` upload: 20 files per account per day (`err_40`).
- **Order numbers.** New orders get `AT-` + 12 hex digits (48 random bits) instead of 8; old numbers stay valid. `syncOperationalProjection` first looks up order IDs that `market_order_records` already holds for another customer and skips them (logged), and both upserts (`market_order_records`, `market_order_finance`) only update a row of the same customer, so one account can never overwrite another customer's order row, fee lines or books.
- **Framing.** `X-Frame-Options: DENY` on every page (`next.config.ts`): the CSP is still report-only, so its `frame-ancestors` did not stop clickjacking.
- **Setup warning.** `GET /api/operations` returns `setupWarnings`; Administration → Система shows them. Today: `ATLAS_AUTH_SECRET` missing (sign-in code hashes then have no secret pepper). Names only, never values.
- **Audit result (items 4–15 of the owner's stage 1 checklist).** Server permission checks, SQL (bound parameters only), raw HTML (static JSON-LD only), tokens (HttpOnly `__Host-` cookie, nothing in localStorage), admin routes (all check `ATLAS_OPERATOR_EMAIL`), CORS (none, same-origin writes), email verification and object ownership were found sound apart from the items fixed above.

## Accounting, customs at checkout, store discounts, document OCR, delivery days — 6 October 2026

- **Accounting (migration `0009_accounting`).** `lib/market/finance.ts` holds the model, as pure tested functions: Atlas is a purchasing agent, so money for the goods, the store's delivery, customs paid for a customer and refunds is *transit*; Atlas *income* is the fee (service + buyout + conversion), international delivery with its margin (the weighed final shipping once settled), the markup on the Central Bank rate (`quote.fxMarkup`, now stored in each quote; `merchandise × (1 − 1/fxMarkup)`), extra services and ledger income (customs-help fee, other). `market_order_finance` keeps every order's split (rebuilt by `syncOperationalProjection` in its own batch, so a database without the table still syncs the rest); `market_ledger_entries` is the money journal the operator keeps (carrier, payment and bank fees, packaging, salaries, rent, marketing, software, goods paid to stores, refunds, tax paid…), voided with a reason and never deleted. A month's profit = income of orders paid that month + income entries − expense entries; profit tax = profit × `market_settings.accounting.profitTaxRate` (default 15%, to be confirmed with the accountant). Admin → Финансы → Бухгалтерия (`app/accounting-view.tsx`) shows the month, adds and voids entries, sets the rate and downloads three CSVs for Excel (`;`, UTF-8 BOM, decimal comma): monthly summary, orders, ledger (`GET /api/finance?export=summary|orders|ledger&from=&to=`). `/api/finance` is operator-only; writes are same-origin and audited.
- **Customs.** The cart shows a compact estimate (status, one sentence about the allowance); the two choices moved to the checkout's confirmation step: "I have already gone over this month's allowance outside Atlas" (with an optional amount; without one the allowance counts as used up) and, when duty is due, "Atlas helps pay customs" — the fee is now 3% of the goods value (owner's decision), still a request outside the order amount. The rules and sources live on `/customs`.
- **Store discounts everywhere.** `Product.sourceReferencePrice` is the store's "before the discount" price in the store currency, display only and never in a total; `storeDiscount()` returns the crossed price and the percentage only when it is above the price and at most 10 times it (the server drops anything else in `checkedCartProduct`). It comes from the catalog card (`referenceUsd`, current prices only) or from a Shopify import (`compare_at_price`, per option), and is shown crossed out with the −N% pill on the order page, in the cart and in the product sheet (`WasPrice`).
- **Passport and ID card OCR.** The old reader relied on the experimental `TextDetector` API (absent in normal Chrome and in Safari) and on passports only. Now `app/passport-ocr.ts` runs Tesseract.js in the browser from `/ocr/` (`scripts/copy-ocr-assets.mjs` copies the worker, the LSTM cores and the English model from node_modules before `npm run dev`/`build`; `public/ocr/` is not committed) — nothing leaves the device. `lib/market/mrz.ts` reads a passport (TD3) or the Uzbek ID card's back (TD1: `I<UZB…`, three lines), finds the lines by their shape (OCR shortens `<` fillers), checks ICAO check digits and corrects letter/digit confusions in the numeric part. iPhone HEIC and very large photos become JPEG before upload. The customer still reviews every field.
- **Delivery days in the admin.** `pricing.deliveryDays` (per region `[from, to]` business days) is edited next to the per-country $/kg in Тарифы; `deliveryDaysFor()` falls back to `site-content.ts`. The home rates table, the example bill and the FAQ read it.
- **Also.** The rate line under bills, the example's store-delivery row and the "estimate, not an offer" footnotes were removed (owner); the order page no longer overflows phones (a stretched screen-reader-only radio; `.sr-only` is now enforced), and e2e snapshots the order page with a catalog product loaded; the cabinet allowance is an interactive bar (`app/allowance-meter.tsx`: order segments, the cart dashed, the limit mark, tap/hover/focus to read a part); address lines show the postal code; the passport page no longer claims only the customer sees the scan.

## Order calculation: hold, weight, fee, rate, customs, stock and notes — 5 October 2026

- **Weight.** `combinedShipmentWeight` = boxed weight of the parcel + `packagingKg` (0.3 kg) once, rounded up to 10 g, at least 1 kg; the former extra 0.2 kg is gone (`paddedWeight` for one item follows). A shipping weight the store publishes (`weightKind: "shipping"`) is used and recorded as `Product.weightBasis = "store"`; otherwise `estimateBoxedWeight(category, title)` (`lib/market/weight.ts`) gives an editable estimate from the kind of item named in the title (boots, t-shirt, laptop…) or the category, labelled as an estimate. Category estimates were lowered to typical boxed weights (shoes 1.2, clothing 0.6 kg…). The server uses a store-stated shipping weight at `cart-add` unless the customer entered their own.
- **Atlas fee and rate.** `atlasServiceFee` = 9.98% of merchandise only. `Pricing.fxSource` (`cbu` | `manual`), `fxMarkup` (1.012), `fxCbuRate`, `fxCbuDate`, `fxUpdatedAt`: with `cbu`, `normalizePricing` sets `fx = round(CBU USD rate × markup)`. `lib/market/fx.ts` parses the official CBU JSON (`https://cbu.uz/ru/arkhiv-kursov-valyut/json/USD/`); `refreshCbuFx` (server) reads it in the background when the tariff is older than 6 hours (D1 lease `atlas:fx-refresh:lease`, 2-minute retry pause, conditional write) and on the operator's "Refresh the CBU rate" (`POST /api/operations` kind `fx-refresh`). A changed soum rate is a new tariff version (`cbu-<date>-<ms>`), so carts quoted at the old rate are shown again. Customers see the rate and when it was read (`FxNote`); without a CBU reading it is labelled a set rate, not the live CBU rate.
- **Owner decisions on stored tariffs.** `upgradePricing` applies revision 2 (`pricingRevision`) to rows saved before it: $15/kg, 9.98%, CBU × 1.012, version `<old>+r2`. Saving the tariff form stores `revision: 2`.
- **Store delivery.** `storeShippingUsd` charges only a stated store delivery. Unknown delivery: free when items from that store cost strictly more than `storeShippingFreeFromUsd` ($50), else a hold (`storeShippingHoldUsd`, `Quote.storeShippingHold`, split by merchandise across the store's lines) that is never part of `quote.total` or the payment amount (`holdOf`). Order history says the hold is kept apart. `confirmStoreShipping` for orders with a hold: within it nothing new is approved and `released` = the unused part (no balance credit, nothing was paid); above it (or with free delivery) only the difference becomes an extra for approval. Older orders keep the former included-reserve settlement.
- **Several options, stock and notes.** `cart-add` takes `quantity` and `note`; `cart-add-many` adds several options of one product (one store check per URL in `/api/actions`), each as its own cart line with its own price and quantity. `Product.stockQuantity` / `stockMoreThan` / `stockSource` are set only by the server from the live store: the eBay Browse API `estimatedAvailabilities` (`ebayStock`: `estimatedAvailableQuantity`, or `availabilityThresholdType: MORE_THAN`); Shopify and the other adapters report only in stock / out of stock, so stock stays unknown there. `maxLineQuantity` caps quantity in `addToCart`, `changeQuantity`, `checkoutCart` and `cart-check` (`sourceIssue.kind = "stock"`); a reported 0 blocks the line. `CartItem.note` / `Order.note` (500 chars, `cart-note`) survive repricing and appear to operators; they are never sent to stores.
- **Customs (`lib/market/allowance.ts`, `lib/market/customs.ts`).** Checked on lex.uz on 2026-10-04: CM resolution No. 244 (courier imports for an individual duty-free up to $200 "within one calendar month", payment on the part above the norm), PP-4508 consolidated (20%, at least $2/kg) and UP-174 §8 (the same rate "from 2027-01-01") — the date conflict is shown to customers. The allowance belongs to a person: orders match by recipient name and, when both sides have one, the masked passport. Only bought (status ≥ 1), paid, non-cancelled orders count, in the calendar month of import (delivery month once delivered, else the current month). `cartCustomsEstimate` adds the customer's "used part of the allowance outside Atlas" (amount optional; without an amount the allowance counts as used up) and the optional "Atlas helps pay customs" request (`customsHelpFee`, 3% of the dutiable value; a request, never charged). `State.cartCustoms` (`cart-customs`) keeps the choices; checkout stores the server's estimate in `Order.customs`. Overrides: `Pricing.customsAllowanceUsd/customsRate/customsMinimumPerKg/customsHelpFee`.
- **Interface.** `app/calc-summary.tsx` (`CalcLines`, `HoldNote`, `FxNote`, `CustomsPanel`) and `lib/market/calc-copy.ts` are shared by the link-order page and the cart: items, Atlas fee, buyout and conversion when set, store delivery (stated / free / held separately), international delivery with the parcel weight, its reserve, extra services, total to pay, then apart the hold, customs and the rate. The link-order page: compact store link (domain and path, full address on click), no import technicalities, option buttons with several picks and quantities ("The only available option" when there is one), stock from eBay, data in labelled blocks (price, store delivery, dispatch country, weight with its basis), an order comment, and a "added — next item / go to cart" panel instead of leaving the page. Orders: a notice per recipient without a linked passport (worded as a placed request while payments are simulated), the hold, comment and customs estimate; operators see the comment and customs too.
- **Speed.** `/api/actions` reads the customer status, account and settings in parallel. In the cart, service normalization sends one fix at a time and never while the customer saves a service (a request in flight used to swallow the customer's tap); the saving service shows "Saving…"; service units save on blur, not per keystroke.
## Checkout, phone field and form controls — 4 October 2026

## Live price check with the stores, tariff guard and cart — 4 October 2026

- **Snapshot comparison.** `compareProductSnapshot(product, variant, extracted)` (`lib/importer/verify.ts`) returns `same`, `changed` (store price, or store delivery the page states — never Atlas's editable reserve — with the old and new values) or `blocked` (`currency`, `variant`, `price`). `verifyProductSnapshot` keeps the old throwing form on top of it. Successful checks stamp `product.sourceCheckedAt` (new optional field); `cart-add` clears whatever the client sent there.
- **Cart check.** `checkCartSources(cart, fetchSource, pricing)` (`lib/market/cart-check.ts`, tested with a fake fetch in `tests/cart-check.test.mjs`) fetches each product URL of a supported store once, skips lines checked in the last 2 minutes and, after a failure, waits 30 s before asking that store again. A changed price is written into the line (USD recomputed with the managed rates) and marked with `priceChange {previousPrice, price, currency, previousShipping?, shipping?, at}`; problems are marked with `sourceIssue {kind, at}`. A store that does not answer blocks a line unless its price was confirmed in the last 30 minutes (then checkout goes on and the order history says so); buyer-confirmed manual lines from stores that hide their data behave as before. Both cart-line fields are optional, so old documents parse.
- **`/api/actions`.** `cart-add` refuses a price that moved since the page loaded with `err_34`; the link-order page then reloads the store data and re-selects the chosen option. New action `cart-check` (sent when the customer presses "Check out") and `checkout` run the cart check; on a changed price (`err_35`), a line to load again (`err_36`), a changed tariff — any quote whose `tariffVersion` differs from the current pricing — (`err_37`) or an unreachable store (`err_38`) the route reprices the cart, saves it and answers 409 with the new state, so the customer sees the new total and the marks instead of an old total passing. When only the 15-minute hold expired and the renewed amounts are identical, checkout re-signs the cart and continues. `checkoutCart` itself also rejects an old tariff version and blocking issues, and writes "price changed before checkout", "checked with the store" or "store did not answer" into the order history.
- **Errors reach the customer.** `act()` showed only a generic "could not save" for domain errors; it now shows the server's text (or the localized code) and keeps the code for the page (`lastActionError()` in the market context).
- **State guard.** `persist()` parses the document before writing it; a state the next read could not parse is refused instead of locking the customer out (the first draft of `priceChange` used the integer soum schema for store prices with cents — caught by this local check and fixed with `storeAmount`).
- **Cart.** "Check out" shows "Checking prices with the stores…" while `cart-check` runs and opens the form only when nothing changed; lines show the result of the last check (new price, "option no longer listed — open item", "store did not answer", or "price checked with the store at 14:32"). The quantity stepper saves after a 400 ms pause, so quick taps become one request instead of being dropped while the previous one saves; the price-hold timer ticks in its own component instead of re-rendering the whole cart every 15 s; after the hold expires the cart says the total is recalculated at checkout instead of asking for a manual refresh.

## Link import fallback and eBay

Automated merchant fetches remain restricted to exact allowlisted HTTPS storefront hosts, with redirect checks, request timeouts and response-size limits. A separate URL-shape validator accepts a public HTTPS product URL for a manual order without fetching an unsupported host; cart-add and checkout can skip a failed merchant check only after the customer marks the entered details as reviewed. The server still recomputes currency conversion, weight and quote fields. Unsafe URLs, credentials, ports, private IP literals and local hostnames are rejected.

`POST /api/actions` must preserve that manual-store boundary: cart-add validates the public URL and skips `fetchProduct()` only for a supported-host miss with explicit manual confirmation. Supported stores still go through live price/currency/option verification. Checkout applies the same rule to new items and treats the optional confirmation bit as true only for compatible legacy cart snapshots; all URLs are still revalidated and checkout recalculates pricing from server settings.

eBay country storefronts are explicitly listed. If public parsing, an eBay redirect, or a recoverable eBay response fails, customer link ordering receives the normal manual-entry form; a definitive not-found response remains an error. Operator batch import saves a reviewable draft from any partial public fields, or an empty draft when the listing is inaccessible. It never publishes that draft or replaces an existing item's saved data with an empty response. eBay automation is limited to data exposed by the public page; no challenge or access control is bypassed.

When configured, `lib/importer/ebay.ts` prefers the official Browse API for numeric eBay `/itm/.../{legacyItemId}` links on supported marketplaces. `EBAY_CLIENT_ID` and secret `EBAY_CLIENT_SECRET` mint a cached application OAuth token using the client-credentials grant; `EBAY_ENV` must explicitly be `sandbox` or `production`. The adapter calls only eBay's fixed OAuth, exact legacy-item and exact seller-variation-group endpoints, supplies `X-EBAY-C-MARKETPLACE-ID`, confirms response item IDs match the requested legacy listing/group, and accepts only fixed-price offers with a positive amount and currency. Group prices stay attached to exact variation IDs; seller `localizedAspects` provide labels, and known-out-of-stock options are excluded. It does not infer centimeter or cross-brand sizing. Seller `itemLocation.country` is used as dispatch country; missing/unsupported locations require the customer's manual country selection instead of assuming the eBay marketplace is the dispatch origin. eBay API traffic bypasses the store-page proxy through a narrow exact-host/path/method gate so OAuth credentials and bearer tokens are not proxied. There is no database/schema change; existing ten-minute import caching and server price/option rechecks remain.

Developer profile completion and production keys do not by themselves establish that a live Browse request succeeds. Production values are configured for a read-only exact-listing check; customer ordering keeps the manual-review fallback until the API returns verified listing data. Import diagnostics log only a fixed stage and HTTP status (or `network`/`missing`), never the source URL, upstream response body, credentials, bearer token, cookies or customer data. Runtime bindings remain optional; missing configuration, unsupported/short links and recoverable API failures preserve the existing fallback.

Nike's public `__NEXT_DATA__` parser matches the article code in the supplied product URL against the style/SKU or an exact Nike PDP path, including object-shaped `pdpUrl.url` and `pdpUrl.path`. It then reads sibling colorways only from that exact matching `productGroups` group; other groups are never selectable. Variant records retain each sibling's size ID/GTIN, price, color, availability signal and safe image for catalog review. The customer order-by-link view filters choices and its gallery to the exact URL-selected article/color. The gallery keeps one preferred rendition per source slot. Only USD men's shoes from Nike's `www.nike.com` storefront get the `Nike US men` size-system marker; the link-order UI uses that marker to label the size grid and link Nike's official conversion chart. Generic size labels and all saved cart/order schemas remain compatible.

The operator `/operations` queue continues to use its existing operator-protected `/api/operations` response and same-origin, revision-checked actions. Its order search reads already-returned order/account fields only; the expanded order panel separates account email/profile phone from the delivery recipient/phone. `mailto:` and validated `tel:` links require an explicit operator click and do not send email/SMS themselves. The profile phone is explicitly marked unverified. No new persisted fields, roles, staff authorization, or API write actions were added.

Operator catalog import applies the same safe fallback to any supported store when `fetchProduct` returns `ManualEntryFallbackError` or times out. The fallback may retain safe partial identity/image data but clears price, currency, variant prices and availability. Existing entries retain their saved fields when a recheck fails. The admin variant matrix permits explicit operator editing, while `catalogIssues` blocks publication if price/currency, variant label, or confirmed available option is missing. A manual operator assertion is not an automated merchant check; public listings must remain unpublished until reviewed.

Catalog descriptions no longer receive generated Atlas boilerplate. Known legacy boilerplate is omitted from public catalog projection and shown blank in the admin editor; custom descriptions remain untouched.

The `/admin` route is the single operator UI for catalog administration, rules, audit/system tools and centrally managed pricing. Global FX/freight/fees, actual-dispatch-country overrides, currency rates and the warehouse service catalogue are loaded and saved through the existing `app/api/operations` endpoints. Both the route content and API reads/writes require the primary operator configured by `ATLAS_OPERATOR_EMAIL`; the UI's staff directory does not grant access. `/operations` is reserved for order processing and no longer presents a duplicate tariff editor. Pricing remains versioned in the existing `market_settings.pricing` record, without a schema migration; orders retain their pricing snapshots.

## Sign-in and sessions

Atlas no longer relies on ChatGPT Sites identity headers; `oai-authenticated-*` headers are ignored. `/login` offers each method only when its provider settings exist (`GET /api/auth/methods`):

- Email code (Resend) and phone code (Eskiz.uz, +998 only): `POST /api/auth/otp` with `step: start|verify`. Six-digit codes live 10 minutes, are stored only as an HMAC bound to the challenge ID (pepper `ATLAS_AUTH_SECRET`), allow 5 attempts, and are deleted on use. Sending is limited to 3 per target per 10 minutes, 10 per day, and 20 per edge IP per hour.
- Telegram Login Widget: the widget's JS callback posts to `POST /api/auth/telegram`; the server checks the HMAC with `TELEGRAM_BOT_TOKEN` and a 10-minute `auth_date` window.
- Google: `GET /api/auth/google` starts OAuth with PKCE, state and nonce; the state is also kept in a short `__Host-atlas_oauth` cookie to block login CSRF. The callback accepts only a verified email with matching issuer, audience, nonce and expiry.

User IDs: email and Google sign-ins map to `email:<address>`, which keeps accounts created under the former ChatGPT sign-in. Phone uses `phone:+998…` and Telegram `tg:<id>`; neither carries an email, so `operator()` can only match `ATLAS_OPERATOR_EMAIL` through a verified email. A successful sign-in creates a random 256-bit token in the `__Host-atlas_session` cookie (HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Lax); D1 `market_auth_sessions` keeps only its SHA-256. Since 5 October 2026 a session lasts 60 days from the last visit: `GET /api/account` (read on every visit) moves `expires_at` forward and sets the cookie again, at most once a day (`renewSession`). `POST /api/auth/logout` deletes the row.

Several methods, one account (5 October 2026, migration `0008_auth_links`): `market_auth_links` maps a sign-in subject (`email:…`, `phone:…`, `tg:…`) to the account it opens; `createSession` resolves it, and without the table (migration not yet applied) everyone signs in to their own method's account as before. In the cabinet ("Способы входа", `app/sign-in-methods.tsx`) a signed-in customer attaches Telegram, phone, email or Google by passing that method's normal check: `POST /api/auth/otp` verify and `POST /api/auth/telegram` with `link: true`, `GET /api/auth/google?link=1` (challenge kind `google-link`, the callback attaches to the account signed in in the same browser and returns `?linked=google` or `?link_error=<code>`). Attaching is refused when the method already opens this account (`link_same`), another account (`link_taken`), or has its own account with orders, balance, cart, favorites, recipients, passports, declarations or support requests (`link_has_account`), so no data is orphaned; sessions of that method's empty account move to this one. `GET/POST /api/auth/links` lists methods and detaches one (same-origin). The operator is still recognised only through the email of the method used to sign in.

Staying signed in: `MarketProvider` treats only a repeated 401 as signed out. A network error, timeout or 5xx while signed in keeps the account on screen and retries (2, 4, 8… s, up to 5 times); a single 401 is asked again after 1.5 s. The account is re-read on focus, `visibilitychange` and `online`, at most every 10 s. `www.atlasmarket.uz` is redirected to `atlasmarket.uz` (middleware), because the session cookie belongs to one host. Eskiz SMS signs in again and resends once when its token is revoked. All auth POSTs require same-origin requests. Dev servers on loopback hosts return codes on screen instead of sending them; production builds compile that path out unless `ATLAS_AUTH_DEV_CODES=true`, and even then only for loopback hosts.

MarketProvider loads account state and revision, then sends action plus expected revision. The server parses Zod action input, applies domain function and persists only when revision matches. Conflict returns current state rather than overwriting another tab.

The root `AtlasThemeProvider` uses `next-themes` with the root `data-theme` attribute, light as the initial default independent of the operating-system preference, and the device-local `atlas-theme` preference key. The localized `ThemeToggle` lives in the shared Marketplace header. Theme choice is presentational only and does not enter customer account state or server requests.

Catalog card rules are finalized in `app/experience.css` after the base catalog styles so the full card retains its surface, radius, image crop and text padding. Long product names and price rows may wrap within grid columns. `app/dark-theme.css` also themes the separate finds search/filter panel and its embedded controls; no catalog data or behavior changes.

Catalog filtering remains a client-side projection in `app/deals-feed.tsx` over the current published list: search and category chips stay visible, sort remains separate, and country/delivered-budget choices sit in a compact `details` panel. Active hidden filters are shown as removable chips; the displayed count excludes visible category/search controls and never renders as zero. `app/finds.css` provides the responsive light layout and `app/dark-theme.css` the graphite surfaces. This presentation layer does not change `filterDeals`, API requests, price formulas or persistence.

The public supported-store strip is an always-visible, localized section in `app/supported-stores.tsx`, not a disclosure or a second link-order callout. Keep one section heading, small store chips and one route to the complete `/stores` directory. Use Atlas forest green for Atlas actions/active controls; retain blue where it helps identify navigation or off-site merchant links. The graphite layer must supply its own readable surface, border, copy and semantic accent colors.

Warehouse services use the normal authenticated action path. At checkout the customer can flag `checkout`-stage preferences; checkout snapshots localized service text, unit, configured rate, dispatch country and request origin without changing the initial quote or authorizing work. For a damaged or mismatched warehouse inspection, `receiveOrder` accepts only a post-inspection approved substitution explicitly marked `resolvesWarehouseIssue` with a proposed replacement value; an unrelated `warehouse-service` change cannot clear the hold.

Multiple recipient passports extend the existing JSON state compatibly: `identityProfiles` is optional, while the legacy `identityProfile` remains a latest-confirmation mirror. Each confirmed masked identity may carry an optional `recipientProfileId`; the scan bytes continue to use the owner-scoped private passport object path. `identity-confirm` validates the recipient ID against the authenticated account's saved addresses, and the D1 document status update stores only the matching masked identity. Each order optionally snapshots `deliveryProfileId` and the matching masked identity alongside its delivery address. Old orders have neither field and continue to use compatible legacy fallback state.

Declaration previews derive identity and address from the selected order snapshot (or compatible legacy state) and reject a mixed-recipient batch. Removing an address unlinks its current profile identity but leaves immutable historical order/declaration snapshots intact. The account no longer exposes customer JSON export/download; operator-only D1 backup export is a separate operational feature and is unchanged.
